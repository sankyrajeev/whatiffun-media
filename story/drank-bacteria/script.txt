A doctor drank a cup of bacteria to prove everyone wrong. In the early nineteen eighties, doctors were sure stomach ulcers came from stress and too much acid. Two doctors in Australia, Robin Warren and Barry Marshall, kept finding a tiny spiral bacterium in the stomachs of ulcer patients. Nobody believed them. So in July nineteen eighty-four, Barry Marshall, aged thirty-two, mixed the bacteria into a broth and drank it. He didn't tell his wife. Within days, his breath began to stink. Then he was vomiting every morning. Ten days in, a camera down his throat showed his stomach was inflamed, and the bacteria were everywhere. A course of antibiotics cleared it. Today, an ulcer that once meant years of pills can be cured in a couple of weeks. And in two thousand five, the doctor who drank his own experiment won the Nobel Prize.
