What if you were trapped four kilometres inside a flooded cave? June twenty-third, twenty eighteen, Thailand. Twelve boys from a football team and their coach walk into Tham Luang cave after practice. Then the monsoon rain comes early. The water rises behind them, and the way out is gone. For nine days, nobody knows if they're alive. Thousands of volunteers pump water and search the mountain. Then two British divers surface in a dark chamber and start counting. Thirteen. All alive. But the way out is hours of diving through black, narrow tunnels, and the boys can't swim it. Saman Kunan, a former Navy SEAL, runs out of air carrying tanks in, and dies. So they try a wild plan. A doctor puts each boy to sleep, in a wetsuit and a full face mask, and divers carry them out one by one. Four boys. Then four more. Then the last four and their coach. All thirteen came out alive.
