What if a rock from space hit you during a nap? November thirtieth, nineteen fifty-four. Sylacauga, Alabama. Ann Hodges is asleep on her sofa, under a quilt. A fireball streaks across three states. A black rock, four kilos, punches straight through her roof. It bounces off her radio and slams into her side. She wakes up with a huge bruise. By nightfall, two hundred reporters are outside her house. The police take the rock. Then the Air Force takes it. Then her landlady sues. It landed on her house, she says, so it's hers. Ann pays her five hundred dollars and finally gets it back. The next day, a farmer finds a second piece lying in a dirt road. He sells it and buys a car and a house. Ann gives hers to a museum.
