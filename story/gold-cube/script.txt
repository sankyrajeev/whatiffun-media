What if you melted all the gold ever mined into one cube? In all of history, we've dug up about two hundred and twenty-two thousand tonnes of gold. Two thirds of it since nineteen fifty. Melt it down, pour it into one block, and the cube is only twenty-two and a half metres on each side. About as tall as a seven-storey building. Small, but heavy. It weighs as much as twenty-two Eiffel Towers. At today's price, it's worth about twenty-five trillion dollars. Now share it out. Every person on Earth gets twenty-seven grams. A little lump worth about three thousand dollars. Want more? There's gold in the sea. In the nineteen twenties, the chemist Fritz Haber tried to pull it out of seawater to pay Germany's war debts.
