What if a chicken lost its head and just kept living? September tenth, nineteen forty-five. Fruita, Colorado. Farmer Lloyd Olsen picks a rooster for dinner. His mother-in-law loves the neck, so he aims the axe high. The head comes off. And the rooster gets up and wanders away. The blade had missed the brain stem, the part that runs breathing and digestion. So the Olsens fed him milk and water with an eyedropper, and cleared his throat with a syringe. They named him Mike. Mike went on tour across America. People paid twenty-five cents to see him. Time and Life magazines wrote about him. He lived for eighteen months with no head. Then, in March nineteen forty-seven, in a Phoenix motel, Mike choked in the night. The syringe had been left behind at the show.
