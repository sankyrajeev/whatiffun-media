What if your spaceship blew up on the way to the Moon? April 1970. Apollo 13 is three hundred thousand kilometres from Earth when an oxygen tank explodes. Power and oxygen start leaking into space. The three astronauts climb into their little moon lander. It was built for two people for two days. Now it has to keep three alive for four. They switch almost everything off, and the cabin drops to three degrees. Then the air starts turning poisonous. Their own breath is filling it with carbon dioxide. The spare filters are square, and the holes are round. So engineers on the ground build a fix out of plastic bags, cardboard and tape, and read it up to them step by step. It works. They swing around the far side of the Moon, four hundred thousand kilometres from home, the farthest humans had ever been. On April the seventeenth, they splash down in the Pacific. All three alive.
