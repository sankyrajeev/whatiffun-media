# What if your spaceship blew up on the way to the Moon? (STORY A, 2026-11-25, category: Rescue)
Apollo 13, April 11–17, 1970 (Lovell, Swigert, Haise).

## Facts (>= 2 sources each)
- Oxygen tank 2 exploded on April 13, 1970, ~56 h into the flight, ~200,000 miles (~322,000 km) from Earth ("three hundred thousand kilometres" in the script, rounded down).
  - https://www.nasa.gov/history/houston-weve-had-a-problem/ ("some 205,000 miles from Earth")
  - https://www.history.com/articles/apollo-13 ("over 200,000 miles")
  - https://www.space.com/17250-apollo-13-facts.html ("nearly 322,000 kilometers")
- The lunar module Aquarius was built for two people for two days; it had to keep three alive for four.
  - https://www.nasa.gov/history/houston-weve-had-a-problem/ ("could support two crewmembers for two days" / "three crewmembers for four days")
  - https://www.history.com/articles/apollo-13 ("two people for 45 hours" / "at least 90 hours")
- Powered down; cabin fell to 38 °F (3.3 °C).
  - https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-350/ch-13-4.html ("temperature dropped to 38° F")
  - https://www.history.com/articles/apollo-13 ("a few degrees above freezing"); Space.com "close to freezing"
- CO2 build-up; command-module lithium hydroxide canisters were square, the LM sockets round; the "mailbox" fix from plastic bags, cardboard and tape, read up from the ground.
  - https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-350/ch-13-4.html ("square ... would not fit the round openings"; "plastic bags, cardboard, and tape")
  - https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/putting-square-peg-round-hole
  - https://www.nasa.gov/history/afj/ap13fj/15day4-mailbox.html
- Farthest humans had been from Earth: 400,171 km (248,655 miles) on April 15, 1970, behind the Moon.
  - https://www.history.com/articles/apollo-13
  - https://www.nasa.gov/history/houston-weve-had-a-problem/ ("distance record of 248,655 miles")
- Splashdown April 17, 1970, South Pacific, all three alive.
  - https://www.space.com/17250-apollo-13-facts.html
  - https://www.history.com/articles/apollo-13
