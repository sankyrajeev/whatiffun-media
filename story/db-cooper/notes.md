# D.B. Cooper (Northwest Orient Flight 305, 1971) — notes

Sources:
- FBI, "A Byte Out of History: D.B. Cooper" (2006, updated 2016): https://www.fbi.gov/news/stories/2006/november/dbcooper_112406
- Britannica, "D.B. Cooper": https://www.britannica.com/biography/D-B-Cooper
- HistoryLink essay 23059 (Casey McNerthney, 2024): https://www.historylink.org/file/23059
- History.com, "Who Was D.B. Cooper?": https://www.history.com/articles/who-was-d-b-cooper

Facts (≥ 2 sources each):
- 24 Nov 1971, the day before Thanksgiving (Britannica, HistoryLink, History.com).
- Northwest Orient Flight 305, Portland → Seattle (all four). Boeing 727 (HistoryLink, History.com).
- Gave name "Dan Cooper"; "D.B." was a press error (FBI, Britannica, HistoryLink). $20 cash one-way ticket (Britannica, HistoryLink; FBI: paid cash).
- Note to attendant: bomb in his briefcase (all four). Briefcase showed wires + red sticks (FBI, Britannica, HistoryLink).
- Demands: $200,000 (in $20 bills) + four parachutes (all four). 36 passengers released in Seattle (all four).
- Flew on toward Mexico (FBI, Britannica, HistoryLink, History.com).
- Jumped from the rear stairs a little after 8 pm (FBI, Britannica, HistoryLink), rain/dark (History.com, HistoryLink), ~10,000 ft (HistoryLink; Britannica: plane kept under 10,000 ft). Loafers + business suit (Britannica; FBI: business suit).
- Never found (all four). 800+ suspects (FBI, Britannica, History.com). FBI stopped active investigation 2016 (all four).
- Feb 1980: Brian Ingram, 8, found $5,800 in rotting $20 bills at Tena Bar on the Columbia River; serial numbers matched (FBI, Britannica, HistoryLink, History.com). 290 bills (HistoryLink).
- "Nine years later": Nov 1971 → Feb 1980 = 8 years 3 months; script says "Eight years later" (first voice take said nine; re-voiced).
