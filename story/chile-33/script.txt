Thirty-three men, seven hundred metres underground, and nobody knew they were alive. On August fifth, twenty ten, a gold and copper mine in the Chilean desert collapsed. The miners crawled into a small emergency shelter. They had food for two or three days. They made it last two weeks. Up top, rescuers drilled blind, hole after hole. For seventeen days, nothing. Then one drill came back up with a note stuck to it. "We are fine in the refuge, the thirty-three." That narrow hole became a lifeline. Water, food and letters went down in tubes. But to get the men out, the rescuers needed a shaft wide enough for a person, and a narrow steel capsule painted in the colours of Chile's flag. Each ride up took about fifteen minutes. On October thirteenth, after sixty-nine days, the last man, Luis Urzúa, stepped out into the night. All thirty-three survived. Subscribe for more rescues.
